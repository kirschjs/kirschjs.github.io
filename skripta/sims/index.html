Chapter I  ·  Polarization: the state as a vector

Stern–Gerlach: the beam splits in two
A silver beam through a rotatable analyzer. Switch between the continuous band a classical magnetic moment would paint and the two spots actually collected, or superimpose them. With the source prepared along m̂ the two frequencies follow P± = (1 ± cos θ)/2 and the running average follows n̂·m̂. A compass shows the transverse plane.

Three filters in a row
SG1 and SG3 along ẑ, a third analyzer between them at any angle, each keeping its + channel. Take the middle one out and the − detector is dead; put it back at 90° and a quarter of the surviving atoms land there. Adding an obstacle raised a count, which no assignment of labels to atoms can do.

The three boxes
The opening puzzle. Anonymous grey boxes, objects with no visible properties, hidden angles, and one control that inserts box B. Nothing on the screen gives the answer away. Press reveal to turn the boxes back into polarizers.

Polarization ellipse
Amplitude ratio and relative phase as sliders. The field vector traces its figure in the transverse plane while the two component traces run below with the phase offset marked. The Jones vector is printed live.

Superposition is not ignorance
Two beams in parallel under one analyzer: 45° photons above, an even mixture of x and y below. Identical at 0°, maximally different at 45°.
